# Problem index

Maps eight general problems to practices in the eight book files, and records where the books disagree. A pointer such as `DW 7` means practice 7 in `deep-work.md`. The line after a pointer only identifies the practice: open the book file for the action, the numbers and the citation before prescribing.

Citations in the book files give the chapter first, with its part or rule where the book has them; `p.` numbers are PDF pages of the copy distilled and vary by edition, so cite the chapter to the user.

Under each problem, **First** ranks the practices to reach for, strongest first, and **Also** lists the others that bear on it. The ranking is editorial; the books do not rank.

## Attention

### `outsourced-thinking`: hands hard thinking to tools or other people

The books give no practice for working with AI agents. The one direct statement is in WE Ch. 5 "Goals" (p. 70): an outcome produced without going through the work brings no fulfilment, because the growth is in the process. Everything below is a practice for thinking unaided; applying it to agents is the mentor's extension and is labelled as such.

**First**
1. `DW 2` Deep work ritual and daily capacity: a fixed place, length and rules for a session, within a daily limit.
2. `SG 6` Deconstruct a hard paper: map what it depends on, then write each step in your own words.
3. `DW 7` Productive meditation: hold one defined problem in mind during a walk.
4. `DW 5` Internet blocks: when stuck and wanting to look something up, wait for the next scheduled block.
5. `FW 12` Expect the discomfort: when the urge to switch to something easier arrives, name it as normal for hard work and return to the task.

**Also:** `DW 6`, `SG 4`, `SG 7`, `FW 11`

### `shallow-focus`: concentration is declining; restless on hard problems

**First**
1. `WE 4` Deep-focus blocks: scheduled, phone out of the room, stray thoughts parked on paper.
2. `DW 2` Deep work ritual and daily capacity.
3. `FW 12` Expect the discomfort and turn back to the task.
4. `DW 5` Internet blocks, at work and at home.
5. `WE 8` Unstimulated waiting: no phone in queues and short gaps.

**Also:** `WE 6`, `DW 1`, `DW 6`, `DW 7`, `SG 4`, `SG 5`, `SP 7`, `FW 10`, `FW 11`, `ES 1`

### `too-many-parallel`: many tasks and projects open at once

**First**
1. `FW 2` Cap work in progress: a fixed number of active items, or one big project at a time; a new one enters only when one leaves.
2. `SP 4` Holding tank: an unlimited waiting list feeding a short active list.
3. `FN 6` Next-goals list: write a new idea down; finishing the current goal is the condition for starting it.
4. `SP 1` Limits at three scales: missions, ongoing projects, and the day's major project.
5. `ES 5` 90 Percent Rule: an option short of a clear yes on its main criterion is a no.

**Also:** `ES 12`, `ES 6`, `ES 7`, `FW 4`, `FW 5`, `DL 9`, `SG 10`, `DW 9`, `WE 3`, `FN 3`

### `no-shutdown`: work runs into evenings and sleep

**First**
1. `DW 4` Shutdown ritual: a fixed end time and a closing routine; work stops there.
2. `FW 3` Fixed start and stop times, decided in advance.
3. `ES 4` Sleep: protected by refusing late tasks.
4. `WE 7` Genuine rest: rest that needs no self-control and no screen; a regular digital sabbath.
5. `FW 6` Stop when the planned block ends, even when the work is going well.

**Also:** `WE 2`, `WE 5`, `WE 9`, `ES 8`, `ES 9`, `SP 6`, `DW 9`, `FW 8`

### `unbounded-side-building`: an enjoyable side activity takes time without limit

**First**
1. `FW 3` Fixed start and stop times, applied to the side activity as well.
2. `SP 11` A bounded stretch: spare time given to one project for a limited period, with a release date; the sacrifice stays temporary.
3. `FN 5` Hiding-place test: do you drift there by accident, need a convoluted reason for it, or has a friend flagged it?
4. `FW 6` Stop when the planned block ends.

**Also:** `FW 8` (see disagreement 6)

## Direction

### `which-skills`: unsure which career skills to build

**First**
1. `SG 3` Read the capital market: winner-take-all (one skill counts) or auction (many mixes work); take the open gates.
2. `DL 4` Good Time Journal: log engagement and energy, then review the log for patterns.
3. `DL 7` Prototype conversations: a short talk with someone already doing the work.
4. `SG 9` Reach the cutting edge of a narrow field before choosing a mission.
5. `ES 5` Extreme criteria: minimum and extreme criteria that an opportunity must pass.

**Also:** `SG 1`, `SG 2`, `SG 8`, `SG 11`, `DL 3`, `DL 5`, `DL 6`, `DL 8`, `SP 9`, `WE 1`, `WE 12`, `FN 9`, `FW 4`

### `dropped-ambitions`: starts, stalls when the next step is unclear, drops it for a new one

**First**
1. `DL 1` Gravity or anchor problem: is the stall a fact to accept, or one failed solution to replace with small tests?
2. `SG 10` Little bets: small, short experiments that return concrete feedback.
3. `ES 11` Slowest hiker: list the obstacles to the next outcome and remove the one that holds up the rest.
4. `FN 2` Cut the goal in half, or double its deadline.
5. `WE 12` Quit, fit, grit: quit freely until a pursuit shows fit; after that, persist. The practice lists the signs of fit.

**Also:** `FN 6`, `WE 5`, `FN 1`, `FN 7`, `FN 10`, `FN 11`, `DL 8`, `DL 10`, `FW 2`, `FW 6`, `FW 7`, `DW 3`, `ES 10`, `SP 10`, `WE 10`

### `crowded-out-life`: exercise, leisure, hobbies and a long-term learning goal are always deferred

**First**
1. `WE 2` Minimum effective doses: a small fixed amount for each neglected area, set in advance.
2. `FW 1` Pay yourself first: the valued activity gets its time before the day's demands.
3. `FW 8` Rest and hobbies done for their own sake, with a rest day prepared in advance.
4. `DL 2` Dashboard: rate health, work, play and love, and look for the empty gauge.
5. `FW 9` Communal commitments: a fixed group activity that the schedule must bend around.

**Also:** `ES 3`, `ES 4`, `DW 8`, `FW 5`, `FN 3`, `SP 5`, `SP 6`, `WE 7`, `WE 9`

## Where the books disagree

Each entry names the two sides and the fact that decides between them.

1. **Explore first, or narrow now.** DL (Ch. 4 to 6) and WE (Ch. 4) say to try several directions before committing: three plans, many prototypes, sampling until something fits. SG (Rule #2 Ch. 7, Rule #4 Ch. 13) and FW (Ch. 4, Ch. 11) say to commit to one thing and treat open options as the trap. **Turns on:** whether a commitment is currently running. During one, the narrowing books apply; at its review date, or with nothing chosen, the exploring books apply.

2. **Quit, or finish.** FN (Ch. 1, Ch. 5) and FW (Appendix) say to finish the one thing, and that a new idea waits. ES (Ch. 12), DL (Ch. 4, Ch. 10) and WE (Ch. 4) say to drop a commitment that would not be chosen again today, or that never fitted, without regard to what it has cost. SP (Ch. 3) allows asking for release from a project that keeps being delayed. **Turns on:** the reason for stopping. A bad day, friction or a newer idea is FN's case. A commitment that fails the from-scratch test, or never fitted, is the others' case; FN itself allows quitting a goal the person does not want (Ch. 6).

3. **How tightly to plan the day.** DW (Rule #4) plans every working minute in blocks of 30 minutes or more; SG (Rule #2, Ch. 6 and 7) gives as its example a subject who budgets the week and logs it in quarter hours. FW (Ch. 7, Ch. 12), SP (Ch. 4) and DL (Introduction) reject tight plans: fix the boundaries, shorten the list, double the estimate. **Turns on:** the failure in front of you. Days that dissolve into shallow activity call for DW; a day already too full calls for FW and SP.

4. **How big the goal should be.** FN (Ch. 2) halves it; FW (Ch. 13) drops grand standards; SG (Rule #4 Ch. 13) says to think small. WE (Ch. 5, Ch. 7) sets the goal just beyond current ability and backs going all in for a season. **Turns on:** the record with this kind of goal. Repeatedly unfinished: halve it. Finished comfortably: stretch it.

5. **Rest as fuel, or rest for itself.** DW (Rule #1, Rule #3) and ES (Ch. 8) justify rest by what it does for work and want leisure planned. FW (Ch. 9) rejects that: rest justified as recovery becomes more work, and a hobby needs no purpose or progress. WE (Ch. 10) is between: rest serves growth, but tracking it spoils it. **Turns on:** little in practice; both sides protect the time. Use FW's framing when leisure has turned into another project to optimise.

6. **An enjoyable diversion: hiding place, or legitimate.** FN (Ch. 5) calls an attractive side task a hiding place when it takes time from the main goal. FW (Ch. 9) defends activity done only for enjoyment. **Turns on:** what it displaces. Taking time from the committed goal or from sleep, it is FN's hiding place; inside its own bounded time, it is FW's hobby.

7. **Interest as a guide to choosing work.** SG (Rule #1) and DW (Ch. 3) reject passion as a guide: choose by what is rare and valuable, and interest follows skill. DL (Ch. 3), WE (Ch. 4), FN (Ch. 4) and ES (Ch. 9) use engagement, curiosity and enjoyment as evidence, though DL and WE also reject a pre-existing passion. **Turns on:** the question asked. Which skill pays off in a field is SG's question; which of several viable options to pick, and whether it can be sustained, is the others'.

8. **Unreachable, or bound to others.** DW (Rule #4) and ES (Ch. 5, Ch. 14) protect time by being hard to reach. FW (Ch. 12) says control over one's own schedule isolates, and recommends fixed commitments with other people. **Turns on:** the kind of time. Hours for concentrated work follow DW and ES; life outside work follows FW.

9. **Remove the distraction, or accept the urge.** DW (Rule #2) and WE (Ch. 8) restrict access: scheduled internet blocks, the phone out of the room. FW (Ch. 6) says such rules rarely hold for long, because the urge to escape a hard task remains and finds another outlet; the remedy is to expect the discomfort. FW grants that limiting the most addictive technology is sensible. **Turns on:** what happens once access is restricted. Restrictions come first; when the restlessness continues with the exits closed, FW's practice applies.
